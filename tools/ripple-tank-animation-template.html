How the bright lines form

The overhead lamp shines through the moving surface and the glass base. Around a crest, the curved water surface bends nearby light rays together. Around a trough, the rays spread apart.

Water surfaceLight rays

Side view through the point source, or perpendicular to the bar. Heights and ray paths are exaggerated to show the idea. The screen is drawn at the same horizontal scale as the water; a real projection can be magnified. On a narrow screen, scroll the side-view diagram horizontally.

A point makes circles

In uniform water, waves travel equally fast in every horizontal direction. Crests emitted at the same time form circles centred on the point source.

A bar makes straight fronts

A straight bar dipping uniformly acts as a line of sources moving together. Away from its ends, the crests are nearly straight and parallel to the bar.

The screen shows light

Turn the lamp off. The water still moves, but the bright pattern disappears. The screen displays an optical projection of the water motion.

This optional animation supplements Section 6; no additional prerequisites beyond waves, crests, frequency, and wavelength are needed. The model uses uniform depth, small waves, a chosen constant speed, and an established wave train. Start-up effects, damping, radial amplitude changes, bar-end diffraction, and edge reflections are omitted. Changing frequency selects a new steady pattern. The optical view illustrates the textbook’s crest-bright arrangement; contrast in real tanks depends on the lighting and screen geometry.
