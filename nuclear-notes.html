1 Discovering radioactivity

Becquerel’s photographic plate

Henri Becquerel was studying whether a uranium compound emitted penetrating radiation after exposure to light. A wrapped photographic plate darkened near the compound even when the compound had been stored in the dark. The observation showed that the emission did not depend on light exciting the sample first.

Marie Curie introduced the term radioactivity for spontaneous emissions from unstable nuclei. The radiation trefoil marks the presence of ionizing radiation or radioactive material. An ionization smoke detector is one application of a small sealed source.

Example · Identify the key control

Why was it useful to keep the uranium compound in the dark? Why was the photographic plate wrapped?

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

Dark storage removed prior illumination as a necessary cause of the emission. Wrapping blocked visible light from exposing the plate. The darkening showed that radiation from the compound could reach the film through its wrapping.

2 Alpha, beta, and gamma radiation

Compare the three emissions

An alpha particle is a helium-4 nucleus with charge +2e. A beta-minus particle is an electron emitted during a nuclear transformation. A gamma ray is a photon from the nucleus. Beta-plus decay, which emits a positron, is a separate mode.

  • Alpha particles lose energy rapidly in matter and have a short range. Paper can stop typical alpha particles.
  • Beta particles generally travel farther than alpha particles of comparable decay energies. Materials such as plastic can absorb many beta particles.
  • Gamma photons are often more penetrating. A thick absorber reduces their intensity. The amount of attenuation depends on photon energy and the material.

Magnetic separation

Rutherford distinguished alpha and beta radiation by their penetration. Paul Villard detected the third component later called gamma radiation. In a transverse magnetic field, charge determines the initial direction of deflection. The curvature also depends on momentum and the field.

Example · Follow the tracks

An initially straight beam separates into two oppositely curved tracks and a straight component. How do the three types of radiation account for that?

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

Positive alpha particles and negative beta-minus particles curve in opposite directions. Neutral gamma photons remain on a straight path. To assign a particular curved track to alpha or beta, use the direction of the field and the original beam.

3 The nucleus, neutrons, and the strong force

What scattering revealed

In the Geiger–Marsden experiment, most alpha particles passed through thin foil with little deflection. A few scattered through large angles. Rutherford explained this with a small, dense, positively charged nucleus containing most of the atom’s mass.

Chadwick studied radiation from beryllium bombarded by alpha particles. It knocked protons out of hydrogen-rich material. Their recoil helped identify a neutral particle with a mass close to the proton’s, the neutron.

How the nucleus stays together

The residual strong nuclear force binds nearby protons and neutrons over distances of order a few femtometres. Protons also repel one another electrically. Changing the neutron-to-proton balance can change nuclear stability.

Example · A neutron on its own

Is a free neutron stable? Write its decay products and check charge.

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

A free neutron undergoes beta-minus decay with a mean lifetime of about 15 minutes. It produces a proton, an electron, and an electron antineutrino.

n → p + e⁻ + ν̄ₑ
Charge: 0 = (+1) + (−1) + 0

Nuclear binding can make a neutron stable inside a stable nucleus.

4 Isotopes and nuclear notation

Read the upper and lower numbers

The upper mass number A counts protons and neutrons together. The lower atomic number Z counts protons and determines the element. The number of neutrons is A − Z. A neutral atom has Z electrons.

Isotopes have the same Z and different neutron counts. Their chemistry is similar because it is mainly governed by electrons. Their masses and nuclear stability can differ. A radioisotope is an isotope that decays spontaneously.

Example · Compare two uranium isotopes

How many protons and neutrons are in ²³⁵₉₂U and ²³⁸₉₂U?

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

Each has 92 protons. Uranium-235 has 143 neutrons and uranium-238 has 146 neutrons. The isotope names use the mass number.

5 Balancing decay equations

Check nucleon number and charge separately

For the nuclear equations in this chapter, the sum of mass numbers A and the sum of charge numbers are the same on both sides. Balance each row independently. The upper zero on an electron or photon means it has no nucleons.

  • Alpha decay lowers A by 4 and Z by 2.
  • Beta-minus decay keeps A fixed and raises Z by 1. A neutron becomes a proton and emits an electron and an electron antineutrino.
  • Gamma emission lowers the nucleus’s excitation energy and leaves A and Z unchanged.

The textbook often omits the antineutrino in simplified beta-minus equations. Include it in a complete equation.

Example · Fill in two daughters

Find the daughter in alpha decay of radium-226 and in beta-minus decay of carbon-14.

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

²²⁶₈₈Ra → ²²²₈₆Rn + ⁴₂He
¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ⁰₋₁e + ν̄ₑ

For the first equation, 226 = 222 + 4 and 88 = 86 + 2. For the second, 14 = 14 + 0 and 6 = 7 − 1. Radon’s atomic number is 86. The printed radon subscript in one quick check is a typo.

6 Decay series and transmutation

Follow the arrows

A daughter can itself be unstable. In a decay series, repeated transformations lead from one parent to a stable end product. Each alpha arrow changes both mass number and atomic number. Each beta-minus arrow changes atomic number while keeping mass number fixed.

A change of atomic number makes a different element. This is transmutation. Nuclear decay can occur spontaneously. A nuclear reaction can also be induced by a collision.

Example · Follow the first two uranium-238 decays

Use the chart to identify the first two daughters of uranium-238.

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

An alpha step changes uranium-238 to thorium-234. A beta-minus step changes thorium-234 to protactinium-234. The chart ends at stable lead-206. Counting each position on the chart gives 15 nuclides from eight elements.

7 Half-life and activity

Halve the parent count repeatedly

A half-life T½ is the time for the expected number of undecayed nuclei to fall to half its starting value. After n half-lives, the fraction remaining is (1/2)ⁿ. An individual nucleus has a probability of decay, while a large population follows a predictable trend.

n = t/T½
N = N₀(1/2)^(t/T½)

Use the same time units for t and T½. N refers to the parent nuclei. The sample can still contain daughter products. A pure parent isotope’s activity is proportional to its remaining count. One becquerel (Bq) is one decay per second.

Sample 9.1.1 · Iodine-131

The textbook uses an iodine-131 half-life of 8.1 days. How long until 1/16 of the parent atoms remain?

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

1/16 = (1/2)⁴, so four half-lives pass. t = 4 × 8.1 = 32.4 days. The printed subscript on the iodine symbol should be 53. Use evaluated isotope data when a real-world half-life is needed. For example, the six-hour technetium half-life belongs to technetium-99m.

8 Investigation 9-1 · Simulating decay

Model a fixed chance of decay

Roll a set of dice. Pick one face to mean decay. Remove the dice that show that face and record the number left. Roll the survivors again. Plot survivors against roll number and include the starting point at roll zero.

A fair die has a 1/6 chance of removal each round. A class total is usually smoother than a single small sample. On a decay curve, read the time where the count reaches half the initial count.

Example · Compare the model with class data

If the class starts with 1000 fair dice and removes one face each roll, find the expected survivor count after four rolls. Estimate the model half-life.

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

Expected count after four rolls = 1000(5/6)⁴ ≈ 482
Half-life in rolls = ln(1/2)/ln(5/6) ≈ 3.8

Read the actual half-life from the class plot at 500 survivors. The experimental value will fluctuate from class to class.

9 Mass defect and energy release

Add all products before comparing masses

A bound nucleus has lower rest mass than its separated nucleons. Its binding energy corresponds to the mass difference. In a reaction, compare the total rest masses of all initial and final particles. Include emitted neutrons and other products when applicable.

Δm = total initial rest mass − total final rest mass
Q = Δmc²
c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s

A positive Q means energy is released, often as kinetic energy or radiation. In the Cockcroft–Walton example, a proton reacting with lithium-7 produces two helium-4 nuclei.

Example · Calculate the reaction energy

The final rest mass in one reaction is 3.00 × 10⁻²⁹ kg less than the initial rest mass. How much energy is released?

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

Q = (3.00 × 10⁻²⁹ kg)(3.00 × 10⁸ m/s)² = 2.70 × 10⁻¹² J per reaction. A macroscopic sample can undergo many reactions, so its total energy can be large.

10 Fission and chain reactions

One fission event can start another

Uranium-235 can absorb a neutron and form an excited uranium-236 nucleus. It can then split into two lighter fragments and release energy and additional neutrons. One possible channel is:

²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴¹₅₆Ba + ⁹²₃₆Kr + 3 ¹₀n + energy

Other fragment pairs are possible. Most of the released energy initially appears as kinetic energy of the fragments and becomes heat in surrounding material.

A chain reaction grows if, on average, more than one neutron from a fission produces another fission. At a steady self-sustaining rate, that average is one. It declines when the average falls below one.

Example · Check the channel

Verify conservation of nucleon number and charge number for the illustrated fission channel.

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

Initial A = 235 + 1 = 236. Final A = 141 + 92 + 3 = 236. Initial charge number = 92. Final charge number = 56 + 36 = 92. The free neutrons contribute nucleons and no charge.

11 Reactors and electricity generation

Trace heat and steam separately

Fuel produces heat. A moderator slows neutrons in a thermal reactor. Neutron absorbers help control the reaction, and a coolant carries heat away. Steam drives a turbine connected to a generator, and the condenser returns steam to liquid water. Shielding and containment help protect people and limit releases. Fuel continues producing some decay heat after shutdown.

  • In a boiling-water reactor, water boils in the vessel and its steam goes to the turbine.
  • In a pressurized-water reactor, the primary water stays liquid and transfers heat through a steam generator to a separate water loop.
  • In a CANDU reactor, separate heavy-water systems moderate neutrons and remove heat from horizontal fuel channels. The primary coolant transfers heat to an ordinary-water steam loop.

Example · Identify the steam path

A diagram shows a reactor vessel connected to a steam generator by a liquid-water loop. A second loop sends steam to the turbine. Is this the BWR or PWR arrangement?

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

It is the PWR arrangement. Water stays liquid in the pressurized primary loop. Heat crosses into the secondary loop in the steam generator, where steam forms. In a BWR the steam comes directly from the reactor vessel.

12 Fusion and stars

Bring light nuclei close together

Fusion joins light nuclei. Positively charged nuclei repel each other, so useful reaction rates require enough relative energy, density, and confinement time. Stars provide confinement through gravity. Over much of a star’s life, nuclear reactions convert hydrogen into helium.

A common example for fusion research is deuterium plus tritium.

²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n + energy

The products have lower total rest mass. Using fusion as an energy source also requires capturing the energy, managing tritium for D–T fuel, and handling neutron effects on materials.

Example · Complete a fusion equation

Deuterium and tritium fuse to produce helium-4 and one other particle. Use conservation to identify it.

Show answerHide answer

Worked answer

The initial A is 2 + 3 = 5 and the initial charge number is 1 + 1 = 2. Helium-4 uses A = 4 and charge number 2. The missing particle has A = 1 and zero charge, so it is a neutron.
