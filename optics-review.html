1 Light and plane mirrors

Key ideas

  • Electromagnetic waves share the same speed in vacuum. A longer vacuum wavelength corresponds to a lower frequency.
  • Draw the normal perpendicular to a reflecting surface. The incident and reflected angles are equal when measured from the normal.
  • A plane mirror forms an upright, same-size virtual image as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
  • A correctly positioned mirror half your height can show your whole body. The extreme rays reflect halfway in height between your eye and your head or feet.

Equations and units

c = fλ ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum
1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m            θᵢ = θᵣ

Check yourself

Q3. You are 1.70 m tall and stand 1.20 m from a flat wall mirror. Find the minimum mirror height for a full-body view and the distance between you and your image.

2 Curved mirrors and ray diagrams

Key ideas

  • A concave mirror converges rays parallel to its principal axis. A convex mirror makes such rays diverge as if from a focus behind the mirror.
  • For a spherical mirror and rays close to the axis, the focal-length magnitude is about half the radius of curvature.
  • For a concave mirror, reflect an axial parallel ray through F. A second ray through C retraces its path. Use the intersection to locate the image tip.
  • Actual rays meet at a real image. Extend diverging rays backward with dashed lines to locate a virtual image.

Q5. Describe the image of a real object in a convex security mirror. Why is this mirror useful in a shop?

3 Mirror equation and magnification

Key ideas

  • Measure dₒ and dᵢ from the mirror vertex. Use f > 0 for concave and f < 0 for convex mirrors.
  • With a real object, positive dᵢ places a real image in front of the mirror. Negative dᵢ places a virtual image behind it.
  • The sign of m gives orientation. Its magnitude gives the image height divided by object height.

Equations

1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ        m = hᵢ/hₒ = −dᵢ/dₒ
dᵢ = 1/(1/f − 1/dₒ)     hᵢ = mhₒ

Check yourself

Q8. A concave mirror with f = 15.0 cm is 10.0 cm from a real object. Find dᵢ and describe the image.

Q9. A concave mirror forms a real image 40.0 cm from its vertex with an object 24.0 cm away. Find f.

4 Refraction and Snell’s law

Key ideas

  • Light slows in a higher-index medium and bends toward the normal at oblique incidence. Frequency remains fixed at the boundary.
  • A ray emerging from a parallel-sided glass slab into the original medium is parallel to the incident ray. It can be displaced sideways.
  • Light from a submerged object bends away from the normal on leaving water. Its backward extensions give a shallower apparent position.

Equations

n = c/v                  n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂
λ = v/f                  Measure all angles from the normal.

Check yourself

Q10. Light travels at 2.00 × 10⁸ m/s in a material. Find its refractive index. What happens to the light’s wavelength on entry from air?

Q11. An air ray strikes glass of index 1.50 at 40.0° to the normal. Find the angle in glass. What angle does it make on leaving the opposite parallel face into air?

Q12. A refraction experiment plots sin θ(air) vertically and sin θ(material) horizontally. The fitted line passes through (0.250, 0.350). Estimate the material’s refractive index and state why the axes use sines.

5 Critical angle, dispersion, and polarization

Key ideas

  • Total internal reflection requires incidence from higher to lower index at an angle greater than the critical angle.
  • Dispersion occurs because refractive index depends on wavelength. In ordinary glass violet bends more than red at the same oblique incidence.
  • A linear polarizer transmits one electric-field direction. A vertical transmission axis can reduce horizontal glare from a road or pond.

Equation and condition

sin θc = n₂/n₁            n₁ > n₂
At θc, the refracted ray is along the interface.

6 Lenses and image formation

Key ideas

  • A converging lens brings parallel rays to a far focus. A diverging lens sends rays out as if they came from the near focus.
  • In the thin-lens model, a ray through the centre travels approximately straight.
  • Positive dᵢ places a real image on the far side from a real object. Negative dᵢ places a virtual image on the object side.

Equations

1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ        m = −dᵢ/dₒ
Converging lens: f > 0   Diverging lens: f < 0

Check yourself

7 Vision and optical instruments

Key ideas

  • The eye focuses light on the retina. A diverging lens corrects typical nearsightedness, and a converging lens corrects typical farsightedness.
  • A camera and a projector form real images. With a fixed camera focal length, focusing on a nearer object increases the required lens-to-sensor distance.
  • A magnifying glass gives an upright virtual image when the object is inside f. A compound microscope and astronomical telescope use an objective to create an intermediate image and an eyepiece to view it.

8 Answers to questions 1–12

Light and plane mirrors · Q1–3

1. f = (3.00 × 10⁸)/(5.00 × 10⁻⁷) = 6.00 × 10¹⁴ Hz. At 650 nm the frequency is lower.

2. θᵢ = 90° − 30° = 60°. θᵣ = 60° on the other side of the normal.

3. The mirror must be at least 0.850 m tall and properly positioned. Your image lies 1.20 m behind the mirror, so you are 2.40 m apart.

Curved mirrors · Q4–6

4. A parallel ray reflects through F. A ray through C retraces its path. They meet between F and C in front of the mirror. The image is real, inverted, and reduced.

5. The convex mirror gives an upright, reduced virtual image behind it. It brings a wide range of directions into the viewer’s field of view.

6. f ≈ 36/2 = 18 cm. Rays from a point source at F reflect in a nearly parallel bundle.

Mirror equation · Q7–9

7. 1/dᵢ = 1/20.0 − 1/60.0 cm⁻¹. dᵢ = +30.0 cm, m = −0.500, and hᵢ = −3.00 cm. The image is real and inverted.

8. 1/dᵢ = 1/15.0 − 1/10.0 = −1/30.0 cm⁻¹. dᵢ = −30.0 cm, a virtual, upright image behind the mirror. m = +3.00.

9. f = 1/(1/24.0 + 1/40.0) = 15.0 cm.

Refraction · Q10–12

10. n = (3.00 × 10⁸)/(2.00 × 10⁸) = 1.50. Frequency stays fixed, so the wavelength becomes 1/1.50, or two-thirds, of its value in air.

11. θ₂ = sin⁻¹(sin 40.0°/1.50) ≈ 25.4°. The ray leaves at 40.0° from the exit normal.

12. n ≈ slope = 0.350/0.250 = 1.40. Snell’s law relates the sines linearly, with slope n(material)/n(air).

9 Answers to questions 13–21

Critical angle, dispersion, and polarization · Q13–15

13. θc = sin⁻¹(1.00/1.50) ≈ 41.8°. The 47.0° ray is above the critical angle and is totally internally reflected.

14. Light refracts and disperses on entry, reflects once inside the drop, then refracts on exit. Red is at the outer edge of the primary bow.

15. Use a vertical transmission axis. Turning it 90° makes the axis horizontal and transmits much more of the reflected light.

Lenses · Q16–18

16. dᵢ = 1/(1/12.0 − 1/18.0) = +36.0 cm. m = −36.0/18.0 = −2.00. The image is real, inverted, and 6.0 cm tall on the opposite side.

17. dᵢ = 1/(1/(−10.0) − 1/20.0) ≈ −6.67 cm. m ≈ +0.333. The virtual image is on the object side, upright and reduced.

18. Put the object inside f. The outgoing rays spread and only the backward extensions meet, so the image is virtual.

Vision and optical instruments · Q19–21

19. This is myopia. A diverging lens spreads the rays before they enter the eye, shifting the focus back to the retina.

20. dᵢ = 1/(1/50.0 − 1/2000) ≈ 51.3 mm. The infinity setting is 50.0 mm, so the sensor distance increases by about 1.28 mm.

21. The image is real and inverted with |m| = 4, so dᵢ = 4dₒ. Then 1/f = 5/(4dₒ). Place the slide 10 cm from the lens and the screen 40 cm away on the other side.
