1 Radioactivity and radiation types

Key ideas

  • Becquerel’s wrapped photographic plate darkened near a uranium compound even after the compound had been stored in the dark.
  • An alpha particle is a helium-4 nucleus with charge +2e. A beta-minus particle is an electron with charge −e. A gamma ray is a neutral photon.
  • In a transverse magnetic field, the two charged components bend in opposite directions. Gamma photons travel straight.
  • Alpha particles generally have the shortest range in matter. Gamma attenuation depends on the photon energy and absorber.

Check yourself

Q1. What did keeping the uranium compound in the dark test? Why was the photographic plate wrapped?

Q2. A beam of alpha, beta-minus, and gamma radiation crosses a magnetic field. Which component goes straight? What happens to the other two tracks?

Q3. Give the composition and charge of an alpha particle. Compare its usual range in matter with that of gamma radiation.

2 The nucleus and isotopes

Key ideas

  • Rutherford explained large-angle alpha scattering with a small, dense, positive nucleus.
  • Atomic number Z counts protons and determines the element. Mass number A counts all protons and neutrons.
  • Isotopes of the same element have the same Z and different numbers of neutrons. A radioisotope has an unstable nucleus.

Counting particles

Protons = Z                 Neutrons = A − Z
Electrons = Z for a neutral atom

3 Balancing nuclear equations

Key ideas

  • Balance the total mass numbers and charge numbers separately.
  • In alpha decay, the daughter has four fewer nucleons and two fewer protons than the parent.
  • In beta-minus decay, a neutron becomes a proton. The electron and electron antineutrino are emitted. The daughter’s A is unchanged and Z increases by one.
  • Gamma emission changes nuclear energy while A and Z remain the same.

Check yourself

4 Half-life and the dice model

Key ideas

  • Half-life is the time for the expected parent count, or the parent’s activity, to halve.
  • Use matching units for elapsed time and half-life. Separate the amount of parent remaining from the amount that has decayed.
  • In the dice model with one decay face, the expected surviving fraction per roll is 5/6. Small samples fluctuate.

Equations

Number of half-lives = t/T½
Parent remaining = initial parent × (1/2)^(t/T½)
1 Bq = one decay per second

Q12. A class starts with 600 fair dice and removes dice that show a particular face after each roll. Find the expected number left after two rolls. How would you read half-life from the class graph?

5 Mass defect and fission

Key ideas

  • Compare total rest masses before and after a reaction, including emitted particles.
  • Fission splits a heavy nucleus. Uranium-235 can absorb a neutron and split into lighter fragments. Energy and additional neutrons are released.
  • A chain reaction is steady when an average of one neutron from each fission produces another fission.

Equation

Q = (total initial rest mass − total final rest mass)c²
c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s

Q15. In a model, an average of 0.80 neutrons from each fission cause subsequent fissions. Will the chain grow, remain steady, or decline? Explain.

6 Nuclear reactors

Key ideas

  • The moderator slows neutrons. Absorbers control how many neutrons can cause further fission. Coolant transports heat.
  • In a BWR, steam forms in the reactor vessel. In a PWR, heat transfers to a separate steam loop.
  • A CANDU reactor uses heavy water to moderate neutrons and a separate pressurized heavy-water coolant circuit.
  • Steam turns a turbine and generator. The condenser turns exhaust steam back into water.

Q18. Why is cooling still needed after shutdown has greatly reduced fission?

7 Fusion and stars

Key ideas

  • Fusion combines light nuclei. Fission splits heavy nuclei. Both can release energy when the products have less total rest mass.
  • Positive nuclei repel one another. Fusion systems need enough relative energy, density, and confinement time to sustain a useful reaction rate.
  • Gravity confines hot matter in stars. In many stars, nuclear reactions convert hydrogen into helium.

Check yourself

Q20. Why does heating a plasma help fusion? Why does temperature alone fail to guarantee a useful energy output?

Q21. Which process powers stars during hydrogen burning? Name one prospective advantage of fusion power and one engineering challenge.

8 Answers to questions 1–12

Radioactivity · Q1–3

1. Dark storage tested whether earlier illumination was necessary. The wrapping blocked visible light while the uranium compound’s penetrating radiation could still reach the plate.

2. Gamma travels straight because it is neutral. Positive alpha and negative beta-minus particles curve in opposite directions.

3. An alpha particle has two protons and two neutrons, with charge +2e. It generally has a much shorter range in matter than gamma radiation.

Isotopes · Q4–6

4. Carbon-14 has 6 protons, 8 neutrons, and 6 electrons when neutral.

5. Both have 92 protons. Uranium-235 has 143 neutrons, and uranium-238 has 146. They are isotopes of uranium.

6. Most of the atom’s volume lies outside the nucleus. The concentrated positive charge in the tiny nucleus can strongly repel a nearby alpha particle.

Nuclear equations · Q7–9

7. ²²⁶₈₈Ra → ²²²₈₆Rn + ⁴₂He. Check 226 = 222 + 4 and 88 = 86 + 2.

8. ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ⁰₋₁e + ν̄ₑ. The charge numbers give 6 = 7 − 1.

9. A = 60 and Z = 28. A gamma photon carries energy and no nucleons or charge, so the daughter remains nickel-60.

Half-life · Q10–12

10. 12/4.0 = 3 half-lives. 800(1/2)³ = 100 parent nuclei expected.

11. 1/32 = (1/2)⁵, so T½ = 25/5 = 5 days.

12. 600(5/6)² ≈ 417 dice. Read the roll number at 300 survivors, using interpolation if needed.

9 Answers to questions 13–21

Mass defect and fission · Q13–15

13. Q = (2.00 × 10⁻²⁹)(3.00 × 10⁸)² = 1.80 × 10⁻¹² J.

14. Initial A = 235 + 1 = 236. 141 + 92 + x = 236, so x = 3. Charge numbers give 92 = 56 + 36.

15. It declines because fewer than one new fission per previous fission occurs on average.

Nuclear reactors · Q16–18

16. A moderator slows neutrons. An absorber captures neutrons. A coolant carries heat away.

17. This is a BWR. In a PWR the primary water stays liquid and heats a separate steam loop through a steam generator.

18. Unstable fission products continue to decay and release decay heat. Heat removal continues after shutdown.

Fusion · Q19–21

19. The missing particle has A = 5 − 4 = 1 and zero charge. It is a neutron, ¹₀n.

20. Heating gives nuclei more kinetic energy so some can approach closely enough to fuse. The reaction rate also depends on density and confinement time, and energy losses affect the net output.

21. Fusion powers hydrogen burning. Potential advantages include a high energy yield per mass of fuel and use of abundant deuterium. Challenges include confining the hot plasma and managing neutron damage in D–T systems.
