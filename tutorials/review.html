Symbols and units

Δ means final minus initial. Subscript 0 or i means initial; f can mean final as a subscript, or frequency as a variable. T denotes tension in a force problem and period in a wave problem; N denotes normal-force magnitude and also appears as the unit symbol for newton. Use the context and units. μₛ and μₖ are dimensionless friction coefficients; μ_linear is string mass per length in kg/m.

Core SI units: m, kg, s, A, K. Derived units: N = kg·m/s²; J = N·m; W = J/s; C = A·s; V = J/C; Ω = V/A; Hz = s⁻¹. Use g = 9.81 m/s² unless another value is assigned, and use 343 m/s for sound in air at approximately 20 °C when appropriate.

Eight mixed problems

Write a diagram, givens, model assumptions, symbolic equation, substitutions, and a checked answer for each.

1. Motion to forces

A 2.00 kg cart moves at +3.00 m/s. A constant net force of +4.00 N acts for 2.00 s. Find final velocity and displacement.

Reveal solution

a = F_net/m = 2.00 m/s². v = 3.00 + 2.00(2.00) = 7.00 m/s. Δx = 3.00(2.00) + ½(2.00)(2.00)² = 10.0 m. Check: F_netΔx = 40.0 J, equal to ½(2.00)(7.00² - 3.00²).

2. Projectile components

A horizontal projectile leaves a 4.90 m platform at 6.00 m/s. Find range and impact velocity with g = 9.81 m/s².

Reveal solution

t = √(2 × 4.90/9.81) = 0.999 s. Range = 6.00t = 6.00 m after rounding. vₓ = 6.00 m/s and vᵧ = -9.80 m/s. Speed = 11.5 m/s, at 58.5° below horizontal.

3. Ramp and friction

A 5.00 kg block already slides down a 30.0° ramp with μₖ = 0.200. Find normal force and acceleration.

Reveal solution

N = 5.00(9.81) cos 30.0° = 42.5 N. fₖ = 8.50 N. Downslope force = 24.525 - 8.496 = 16.0 N. a = 3.21 m/s² downhill. The kinetic model assumes sliding; it does not decide release from rest.

4. Work and energy

A 1.00 kg object starts from rest at height 2.00 m. During descent, the object-track-Earth system gains 3.00 J of thermal energy. Find bottom speed.

Reveal solution

Initial U = 19.62 J. Final K = 19.62 - 3.00 = 16.62 J. v = √(2K/m) = 5.77 m/s. Total energy is conserved; mechanical energy drops by 3.00 J.

5. Thermal equilibrium

An insulated mixture contains 0.100 kg water at 70.0 °C and 0.200 kg at 25.0 °C. Ignore container heat capacity. Find T_f.

Reveal solution

Equal specific heat cancels. T_f = [0.100(70.0) + 0.200(25.0)]/0.300 = 40.0 °C. With c = 4180, Q_hot = -12 540 J and Q_cool = +12 540 J.

6. Circuit and power

A 9.00 V ideal source connects to 3.00 Ω in series with two 6.00 Ω resistors in parallel. Find source current, branch currents, and total power.

Reveal solution

The parallel equivalent is 3.00 Ω; total is 6.00 Ω. Source current = 1.50 A. The series resistor drops 4.50 V, leaving 4.50 V across both branches. Each branch carries 0.750 A. Total power = 9.00 × 1.50 = 13.5 W.

7. Real battery energy rates

A 6.00 V EMF battery with internal resistance 0.500 Ω supplies a 2.50 Ω load. Find terminal voltage and compare energy transfer rates.

Reveal solution

I = 6.00/(2.50 + 0.500) = 2.00 A. Terminal voltage = 5.00 V. Source power = 12.0 W; load power = 10.0 W; internal heating = I²r = 2.00 W. The rates add correctly.

8. Sound resonance and beats

An ideal open-closed pipe has length 0.250 m in air at 343 m/s. Find its fundamental and next allowed harmonic. A 345 Hz tone plays with its fundamental; find beat frequency.

Reveal solution

f₁ = 343/[4(0.250)] = 343 Hz. The next allowed harmonic is f₃ = 1029 Hz ≈ 1.03 × 10³ Hz. Beat frequency = |345 - 343| = 2 Hz, using the assigned source values.

Check your solution

  • Does the equation apply to the physical situation and time interval?
  • Do units cancel correctly, and do the signs match the chosen axes?
  • Is the result plausible in a limiting case?
  • Did you include all relevant forces or energy transfers once?
  • Are precision, uncertainty, and model limitations stated where relevant?
