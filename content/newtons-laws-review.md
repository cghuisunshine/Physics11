# Newton's laws: quick review

## 1 Inertia and Newton's first law
### Key ideas
- **Inertia** is an object's tendency to maintain its velocity. Mass measures inertia: greater mass means more resistance to a change in velocity. Inertia is not a force.
- In an inertial reference frame, an object stays at rest or moves at **constant velocity** when its net force is zero.
- Constant velocity means constant speed **and direction**. Speeding up, slowing down, and turning all require a net force.
- Zero net force does not mean no individual forces. A car moving steadily along a straight road has balanced forces.
- **Mass** is measured in kg; **weight** is gravitational force in N. An astronaut in orbit still has mass and inertia; apparent weightlessness occurs because the astronaut and station fall together.
### Equations to remember
```
F_net = ΣF = 0  ⇒  a = 0  ⇒  constant velocity
Weight: F_g = mg       Earth: g = 9.81 N/kg
```
Zero velocity at one instant does not guarantee zero acceleration. A ball at the top of its flight still accelerates downward.

## 2 Newton's second law
### Key ideas
- The **net force** is the vector sum of all forces on one object. It determines acceleration, rather than velocity.
- For constant mass, acceleration points in the direction of the net force. Force and velocity can point in opposite directions while an object slows down.
- At fixed mass, acceleration is directly proportional to net force. At fixed net force, acceleration is inversely proportional to mass.
- Choose a positive direction before adding forces. Opposing forces have opposite signs; an applied force is not necessarily the net force.
### Equations and units
```
F_net = ma       a = F_net/m       m = F_net/a
1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
```
Use kg, N, and m/s². Convert grams to kg by dividing by 1000. Convert km/h to m/s by dividing by 3.6.
### Direction check
An object moving right with a leftward net force initially slows down. If that force continues after the object stops, the object starts moving left.

## 3 Free-body diagrams and friction
### Key ideas
- Choose one object and draw only forces **acting on it**. Label the source of each force; do not add velocity or inertia as a force.
- Weight acts downward; normal force acts perpendicular to the contact surface. Applied force and friction depend on the situation.
- On a level floor with a horizontal push and no vertical acceleration, **F_N = mg**. An upward component of a pull reduces the normal force.
- Kinetic friction opposes relative sliding. Static friction adjusts to prevent slipping up to a maximum; it is not always at that maximum.
### Equations for a sliding block
```
Vertical balance:       F_N − mg = 0
Kinetic friction:       F_fr = μ_k F_N
Along a horizontal push: F_push − F_fr = ma
Static friction:        0 ≤ F_static ≤ μ_s F_N
```
The first and third equations assume a horizontal push on a level floor, with no vertical acceleration. Draw the forces before using them.

## 4 Testing the second law
### Key ideas
- For a cart and hanging mass joined by a light string over an ideal pulley, include **both masses** in the total accelerating mass.
- On a horizontal track with negligible resistance, the hanging weight drives the combined system. Tension is internal to this system and is less than the hanging weight during downward acceleration.
- To test force at fixed mass, transfer masses from the cart to the hanger. To test mass at fixed driving force, keep the hanging mass fixed and add mass to the cart.
- Use repeated measurements and note friction, pulley inertia, and timing uncertainty. Real measurements need not lie on a perfect line.
### Graphs and timing
```
a vertically vs F_net horizontally: slope = 1/m
a vertically vs m horizontally:     decreasing curve
a vertically vs 1/m horizontally:   slope = F_net
Speed vs time, fixed direction:     slope = acceleration along motion
At 60 Hz: Δt = number of timing intervals / 60
```
The first graph holds mass fixed; the next two hold net force fixed. A timing interval is a **gap between dots**. Convert cm/s² to m/s² by dividing by 100.
### Check yourself
**Q10.** With fixed mass, an a-versus-F_net graph has slope 0.50 (m/s²)/N. Find the mass. With fixed net force, what does the slope of a against 1/m represent?

**Q11.** A 0.800 kg cart is connected to a 0.200 kg hanging mass. The track is horizontal; neglect friction, string mass, and pulley inertia. Find the system acceleration and string tension.

**Q12.** A ticker timer runs at 60 Hz. How much time passes over six gaps? A cart travels straight without reversing. Successive interval speeds are 0.20 m/s and 0.50 m/s, with their midpoint times 0.10 s apart. Find the acceleration.

## 5 Newton's third law
### Key ideas
- When A exerts a force on B, B simultaneously exerts an **equal and opposite** force on A. Both are the same type of interaction.
- The pair acts on **different objects**, so it does not cancel on either object's individual free-body diagram.
- Equal interaction forces do not imply equal accelerations. The lighter object has the greater acceleration magnitude if these are its net forces.
- Weight and normal force on a resting book balance, but are not a third-law pair: both act on the book and have different interaction partners.
### Name the two objects
```
F_(A on B) = −F_(B on A)
Book's weight: Earth pulls book down ↔ book pulls Earth up
Support: table pushes book up ↔ book pushes table down
```
For a horse and cart, the harness forces are internal to the combined system. The ground's forward force on the horse can accelerate the system if it exceeds external resistance. Two spring balances in series at steady tension T each read T.
### Check yourself
**Q13.** A book rests on a table. Identify the reaction partner to (a) Earth's gravitational pull on the book and (b) the table's upward push on the book. Why are weight and normal force not a pair?

**Q14.** Cart A has mass 1.00 kg and cart B has mass 3.00 kg. A pushes B right with 6.00 N. Neglect other horizontal forces. Find the force on A and both accelerations.

**Q15.** Two people pull steadily on opposite ends of two light spring balances connected in series. Each person pulls with 10 N. What does each balance read? Explain why the reading is not 20 N.

## 6 Momentum and impulse
### Key ideas
- **Momentum** is mass times velocity and has direction. Choose a positive direction and use signed velocities.
- **Impulse** equals momentum change. For constant mass, subtract initial velocity from final velocity; a reversal changes both speed and direction.
- The signed area under a net-force-versus-time graph gives impulse. Use average force when the force varies.
- For the same momentum change, increasing stopping time reduces the **average** net force. Padding, airbags, and bending knees use this principle; peak force can differ from average force.
### Equations and units
```
p = mv                      units: kg·m/s
Δp = m(v_f − v_i)
J = F_net,avg Δt = Δp        1 N·s = 1 kg·m/s
F_net,avg = Δp/Δt
```
Momentum alone does not determine injury or damage; contact area, deformation, and energy also matter.

## 7 Conservation of momentum
### Key ideas
- Total system momentum stays constant when the **net external impulse** is zero or negligible over the event, along the axis being considered.
- Include every interacting object in the chosen system. Internal forces transfer momentum between objects; individual momenta can change while the total stays constant.
- If objects stick together, they share one final velocity. Momentum can be conserved even when kinetic energy is not.
- In recoil from rest, final momenta are equal and opposite. A greater mass then has a smaller speed, rather than a greater interaction force.
### Equations and method
```
p_total,before = p_total,after
m_A v_Ai + m_B v_Bi = m_A v_Af + m_B v_Bf
Sticking: v_f = (m_A v_Ai + m_B v_Bi)/(m_A + m_B)
Recoil from rest: m_A v_Af + m_B v_Bf = 0
```
State the system, choose a positive direction, and check external impulse before conserving momentum. Use signed velocities, not just positive speeds.
### Check yourself
**Q19.** A 0.500 kg cart moves right at 4.00 m/s and sticks to a stationary 1.50 kg cart. Neglect external horizontal impulse. Find their common final velocity.

**Q20.** Two carts start at rest and push apart. A 1.00 kg cart moves left at 3.00 m/s. Find the velocity of the other 3.00 kg cart, with negligible external horizontal impulse.

**Q21.** A 2000 kg railway car moves at 3.00 m/s and receives 1000 kg of material falling vertically with zero horizontal velocity. The load stays in the car. Find the final horizontal velocity if external horizontal impulse is negligible.

## 8 Answers: questions 1–12
### Inertia and the first law · Q1–3
**1.** The passenger's inertia maintains their forward velocity until a net force changes it. The seatbelt exerts a backward force on the passenger, slowing them with the car.

**2.** Constant velocity means F_net = 0. Friction is **12 N left**, balancing the pull; **a = 0 m/s²**.

**3.** The initial path is **tangent to the curve** at departure. The track's sideways guiding force ends; the train does not need an outward force to leave the curve.
### The second law · Q4–6
**4.** a = 6.00/3.00 = **2.00 m/s² right**. With twice the mass, a = 6.00/6.00 = **1.00 m/s² right**.

**5.** F_net = ma = (1200)(−2.50) = **−3.00 × 10³ N**, or **3.00 × 10³ N left**. It points opposite the rightward velocity, so the car slows.

**6.** a_new/a = (3F_net/2m)/(F_net/m) = **3/2**. Acceleration becomes **1.5 times** its original magnitude, in the same direction.
### Force diagrams and friction · Q7–9
**7.** Weight down, normal force up, applied push right, kinetic friction left. With no vertical acceleration, **F_N = mg**. The horizontal forces need not balance.

**8.** F_N = (5.00)(9.81) = **49.1 N up**. F_fr = (0.200)(49.05) = **9.81 N left**. F_net = 20.0 − 9.81 = **10.2 N right**. a = 10.19/5.00 = **2.04 m/s² right**.

**9.** F_N + 20.0 − mg = 0. F_N = (10.0)(9.81) − 20.0 = **78.1 N up**. Use this reduced normal force for friction, rather than mg.
### Experiments and graphs · Q10–12
**10.** Slope = 1/m, so m = 1/0.50 = **2.0 kg**. The slope of a against 1/m is **F_net in N**.

**11.** Total mass = 0.800 + 0.200 = 1.000 kg. a = (0.200)(9.81)/1.000 = **1.96 m/s²**, with the hanger downward. For the cart, T = (0.800)(1.962) = **1.57 N**. T is less than the hanging weight, 1.962 N.

**12.** Δt = 6/60 = **0.10 s** (six gaps span seven dots). a = (0.50 − 0.20)/0.10 = **3.0 m/s²**, along the motion if the cart travels straight without reversing.

## 9 Answers: questions 13–21
### Interaction pairs · Q13–15
**13.** (a) The book pulls Earth gravitationally upward toward the book. (b) The book pushes the table downward. Weight and normal force both act on the book; a third-law pair acts on **different objects** with the interaction partners reversed.

**14.** B pushes A with **6.00 N left**. Taking right as positive, a_A = −6.00/1.00 = **−6.00 m/s²**; a_B = +6.00/3.00 = **+2.00 m/s²**. Equal forces give unequal accelerations because the masses differ.

**15.** Each reads **10 N**. Each balance measures the tension. Opposite 10 N forces at its ends balance; their magnitudes are not added to obtain the reading.
### Momentum and impulse · Q16–18
**16.** p_i = (0.200)(−10.0) = **−2.00 kg·m/s** (left). Δp = (0.200)[5.00 − (−10.0)] = **+3.00 kg·m/s** (right).

**17.** J = (0.200)(0 − 10.0) = **−2.00 N·s**. F_net,avg = −2.00/0.100 = **−20.0 N**, both left. Doubling the time leaves the impulse unchanged and halves the average force to **−10.0 N**.

**18.** J = triangular area = ½(0.20)(40) = **4.0 N·s**. F_net,avg = 4.0/0.20 = **20 N**. Δv = J/m = 4.0/0.50 = **8.0 m/s**, all in the positive direction.
### Conserving momentum · Q19–21
**19.** v_f = [(0.500)(4.00) + (1.50)(0)]/(0.500 + 1.50) = **1.00 m/s right**. The two-cart system has negligible external horizontal impulse.

**20.** Initial momentum is zero. (1.00)(−3.00) + (3.00)v_Bf = 0, giving **v_Bf = +1.00 m/s**, or **1.00 m/s right**. The two final momenta are equal and opposite.

**21.** Include the car and incoming material. (2000)(3.00) + (1000)(0) = (3000)v_f, giving **2.00 m/s in the car's original direction**. Horizontal momentum is conserved even though vertical forces act.
