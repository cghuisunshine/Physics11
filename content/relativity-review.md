# Special Relativity quick review

## 1 Reference frames and postulates
### Key ideas
- A frame specifies positions, times, and velocities. In an inertial frame a free object moves at constant velocity.
- For everyday speeds, add signed velocities along the same line and name the frame. Reversing which object is the reference reverses the relative velocity.
- The laws of physics have the same form in every inertial frame. All inertial observers measure the same speed c for light in vacuum.
- Receiving a light signal is a different event from its emission. Light from a distant star carries information about the star when the light left.
### Check yourself
**Q1.** Take downstream as positive. A boat moves at +8.0 km/h relative to water that flows at +3.0 km/h relative to the shore. Find the boat’s shore-relative velocity and the shore’s velocity relative to the boat.

**Q2.** A spacecraft moving at 0.70c sends a light pulse forward in vacuum. How fast is it measured aboard the ship and by an Earth observer?

**Q3.** A star is 12 light-years away in the Earth frame. About how long ago was the light we receive now emitted? Explain.

## 2 The Lorentz factor and time dilation
### Key ideas
- Proper time Δτ is the elapsed time on a clock that is present at both events. The textbook uses t₀.
- A frame in which that clock moves assigns a longer interval Δt. In the light-clock model, the outside frame’s light path is diagonal and longer.
- For a massive object, use |v| < c. The factor γ is 1 at rest and increases as speed increases.
### Equations
```
β = v/c                  γ = 1/√(1 − β²)
Δt = γΔτ                 Δτ = Δt/γ
c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s
```
### Check yourself
**Q4.** At 0.60c, find γ. A light clock records 4.0 s on the ship. Find the outside time interval.

**Q5.** In an Earth frame, 20.0 min elapse while a ship travels steadily at 0.80c. How much time elapses on the ship’s clock?

**Q6.** An onboard clock records 5.0 s while the outside frame assigns 10.0 s to the same pair of events. Find γ and v/c.

## 3 Length contraction and simultaneity
### Key ideas
- Proper length L₀ is measured in the object’s rest frame. To measure a moving object’s length, record its endpoints simultaneously in the measuring frame.
- Contraction is along the direction of motion. A transverse width stays the same.
- Two events at different positions can be simultaneous in one frame and separated in time in another.
### Equations
```
L = L₀/γ = L₀√(1 − β²)
Δt′ = γ(Δt − vΔx/c²), with the primed frame moving at +v
```
### Check yourself
**Q7.** A ship is 120 m long and 8.0 m wide at rest. It passes lengthwise at 0.60c. Find the length and width measured from Earth.

**Q8.** A bus measures 10.0 m at rest and 6.00 m as it passes a laboratory. Find v/c.

**Q9.** Two lamps flash simultaneously in the platform frame. Lamp B is farther along the moving train’s direction than lamp A. Which flash is assigned the earlier time in the train frame?

## 4 Muons and round trips
### Key ideas
- A muon’s proper mean lifetime is defined in its rest frame. Earth assigns a longer mean lifetime to a fast-moving muon.
- The mean travel distance in Earth’s frame is vγΔτ. Individual muons can travel farther or less far than this mean.
- A round-trip traveller switches inertial frames at turnaround. When the clocks reunite, compare elapsed proper times directly. In the idealized model add Δt/γ for each constant-speed leg.
### Check yourself
**Q10.** A muon has a model proper mean lifetime of 2.0 μs and moves at 0.998c. Find its Earth-frame mean lifetime and mean travel distance.

**Q11.** Earth measures 15.0 years for a round trip. The ship cruises at 0.80c on both legs. Neglect time spent turning around. How much does the traveller age?

**Q12.** A traveller wants 500 years to elapse on Earth during an idealized round trip while only 50.0 years elapse on their own clock. What γ and cruising speed are required?

## 5 Relativistic velocity addition
### Key ideas
- For motion along one line, let u′ be an object’s velocity relative to a ship and v the ship’s velocity relative to Earth.
- Choose one positive direction and use signed velocities. For ordinary speeds the denominator is approximately 1.
- For light in vacuum, substituting u′ = c gives u = c for a forward beam.
### Equation
```
u = (u′ + v)/(1 + u′v/c²)
```
### Check yourself
**Q13.** A ship moves at +0.60c. It launches a probe at +0.50c relative to itself. Find the Earth-frame velocity of the probe.

**Q14.** The same ship launches a probe backward at −0.50c relative to itself. Find the Earth-frame velocity and its direction.

**Q15.** The ship in Q13 sends a forward light pulse instead. Show algebraically or numerically that its Earth-frame speed is c.

## 6 Momentum and the speed limit
### Key ideas
- Relativistic momentum is p = γmv, with invariant mass m. At low speed this approaches mv.
- Average net force equals the change in momentum divided by the time interval in the chosen frame.
- For an object with mass, p and total energy grow without bound as v approaches c.
### Equations
```
p/(mc) = γβ              F(avg) = Δp/Δt
E = γmc²
```
### Check yourself
**Q16.** A particle moves at 0.80c. Give its Newtonian and relativistic momenta in units of mc.

**Q17.** An object with invariant mass 1.00 kg accelerates from rest to 0.60c in 3.00 s as measured in the laboratory. Find its average net force in that frame.

**Q18.** Explain why continued acceleration cannot bring an electron to exactly c with finite energy.

## 7 Mass–energy equivalence
### Key ideas
- Rest energy E₀ is present even at zero speed. A moving object’s total energy includes both rest energy and kinetic energy.
- When a system’s rest mass decreases by Δm, the associated released energy is Δmc². Complete conversion is a model used in some of the exercises.
- Convert masses to kilograms. One kilowatt-hour is 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
### Equations
```
E₀ = mc²                 E = γmc²
K = (γ − 1)mc²
```
### Check yourself
**Q19.** Find the rest energy of 1.00 mg of matter in joules.

**Q20.** A particle of rest energy 12.0 J moves at 0.80c. Find its total energy and kinetic energy.

**Q21.** Suppose 2.00 g of mass could be completely converted into electricity. How many kWh would this provide? For a steady use of 600 kWh per month, how many months would it cover?

## 8 Answers to questions 1–12
### Reference frames and postulates · Q1–3
**1.** The boat’s velocity is +8.0 + 3.0 = **+11.0 km/h**. The shore’s velocity relative to the boat is **−11.0 km/h**.

**2.** Both observers measure **c**. This is the second postulate for vacuum light.

**3.** The light was emitted about **12 years ago** in the Earth frame. A light-year is the distance light travels in one year.

### Time dilation · Q4–6
**4.** γ = 1/√(1 − 0.60²) = **1.25**. Δt = (1.25)(4.0 s) = **5.0 s**.

**5.** At 0.80c, γ = 5/3. The onboard proper time is 20.0/(5/3) = **12.0 min**.

**6.** γ = 10.0/5.0 = **2.0**. β = √(1 − 1/γ²) ≈ **0.866**, so v ≈ 0.866c.

### Length contraction · Q7–9
**7.** L = 120√(1 − 0.60²) = **96 m**. The transverse width is still **8.0 m**.

**8.** β = √[1 − (6.00/10.0)²] = **0.800**.

**9.** **B** is earlier. For B minus A, Δt = 0 and vΔx > 0, so Δt′ is negative.

### Muons and round trips · Q10–12
**10.** γ ≈ 15.8, so Δt ≈ **32 μs**. Using the unrounded time gives vΔt ≈ **9.5 km**.

**11.** The traveller ages 15.0/(5/3) = **9.0 years**. Apply the same γ on both cruise legs.

**12.** γ = 500/50.0 = **10.0**. v = c√(1 − 1/10.0²) ≈ **0.995c**.

## 9 Answers to questions 13–21
### Velocity addition · Q13–15
**13.** u = (0.50 + 0.60)c/(1 + 0.50 × 0.60) ≈ **+0.846c**.

**14.** u = (−0.50 + 0.60)c/(1 − 0.50 × 0.60) ≈ **+0.143c**. It still travels in the positive direction in Earth’s frame.

**15.** u = (c + v)/(1 + v/c) = **c**.

### Momentum and the speed limit · Q16–18
**16.** The Newtonian value is **0.80mc**. The relativistic value is (5/3)(0.80)mc ≈ **1.33mc**.

**17.** p = (1.25)(1.00)(0.60)(3.00 × 10⁸) = 2.25 × 10⁸ kg·m/s. F(avg) = p/3.00 = **7.50 × 10⁷ N** in the direction of motion.

**18.** An electron has nonzero invariant mass. As v approaches c, γ and E = γmc² grow without bound. Reaching c would require unbounded energy.

### Mass and energy · Q19–21
**19.** m = 1.00 × 10⁻⁶ kg. E₀ = mc² = **9.00 × 10¹⁰ J**.

**20.** E = (5/3)(12.0) = **20.0 J**. K = 20.0 − 12.0 = **8.00 J**.

**21.** E = (0.00200)(3.00 × 10⁸)² = 1.80 × 10¹⁴ J = **5.00 × 10⁷ kWh**. Divide by 600 kWh/month to get about **8.3 × 10⁴ months**.
