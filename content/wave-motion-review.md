# Wave Motion quick review

## 1 Wave types and measurements
### Key ideas
- A **pulse** is one travelling disturbance. A **periodic wave** repeats regularly. Both carry energy.
- A mechanical wave needs matter. Electromagnetic waves can travel through a vacuum.
- Transverse oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of travel. Longitudinal oscillations are parallel to it.
- Read **amplitude A** from the equilibrium line to a crest or trough. Measure **wavelength λ** from crest to adjacent crest, trough to adjacent trough, or compression to adjacent compression.
### Reading a graph
A displacement–position graph is a snapshot and shows λ. A displacement–time graph tracks one point and shows T. Either graph can show amplitude.
### Check yourself
**Q1.** Give a rope one quick sideways flick, then move the end up and down steadily. What type of wave pattern does each action produce? How does a marked segment move as the pattern passes?

**Q2.** In a wave snapshot, consecutive crests sit at 0.20 m and 0.85 m. Each is 3.0 cm above equilibrium. Find the wavelength, amplitude, and trough-to-crest height for a symmetric wave.

**Q3.** A compression moves right along a spring. In which direction do the coils oscillate? Classify the wave and explain how to measure λ if the compressions repeat regularly.

## 2 Frequency, period, and speed
### Key ideas
- **Frequency f** counts complete cycles per second. **Period T** measures seconds per cycle.
- The wave pattern travels one wavelength during one period.
- At fixed wave speed, doubling the frequency halves the wavelength. Convert quantities to consistent units before substituting.
### Equations and units
```
f = N/Δt = 1/T           T = Δt/N = 1/f
v = fλ = λ/T             λ = v/f
1 Hz = 1 s⁻¹             1 MHz = 10⁶ Hz
1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m            c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum
```
### Check yourself
**Q4.** A spring completes 18 oscillations in 12.0 s. Find its frequency and period.

**Q5.** A 440 Hz sound wave travels at 343 m/s. Find its wavelength. At the same speed, what wavelength would a sound of twice the frequency have?

**Q6.** A sonar signal has frequency 30.0 kHz and wavelength 5.00 cm in water. It returns 0.800 s after emission. Find wave speed and the distance to the reflector.

## 3 Reflection
### Key ideas
- A wave reflects when it returns from a boundary. An incoming ray and its reflected ray make equal angles with the normal, the line perpendicular to the barrier.
- A reflected wave in the same uniform medium retains its speed, wavelength, and frequency. Reflection can change direction and amplitude.
- Circular wavefronts reflected from a flat barrier appear to come from a mirror-image source.
- A fixed end inverts a transverse pulse. Parallel rays incident along a parabolic reflector’s axis reflect toward its focus.
### Check yourself
**Q7.** A ray makes a 25° angle with the surface of a flat barrier. Draw its normal and the incident and reflected rays. Give both angles measured from the normal.

**Q8.** An upward pulse approaches a spring’s fixed end. Sketch it just after reflection and explain its sign.

**Q9.** A small wave source is placed at the focus of a parabolic reflector. Describe the reflected wavefronts. What happens if you reverse the direction of travel of those reflected waves?

## 4 Refraction
### Key ideas
- At a stationary boundary the source fixes the frequency. The wavelength changes in proportion to the wave speed.
- At oblique incidence a ray bends toward the normal on entering a slower region, and away from the normal on entering a faster one.
- Along the normal the ray continues straight through. Speed and crest spacing may still change.
### Equations and method
```
f₂ = f₁                  λ₂/λ₁ = v₂/v₁
```
Draw the normal first. If you see wavefronts in a diagram, draw the rays perpendicular to those fronts.
### Check yourself
**Q10.** A 6.00 Hz ripple has wavelengths 3.00 cm in deep water and 2.00 cm in shallow water. Find both speeds and their ratio v(shallow)/v(deep). What is the frequency in shallow water?

**Q11.** A ray enters the shallow water of Q10 at an angle to the normal. Which way does it bend? Describe its direction if it later exits through a parallel boundary into water with the original depth.

**Q12.** Straight wavefronts are parallel to a boundary when they enter a slower region. What happens to the ray direction, wavelength, and spacing in time between crests?

## 5 Diffraction
### Key ideas
- **Diffraction** spreads waves past an edge or through an opening.
- Spreading is greater when wavelength is large relative to the opening. Through a narrow opening, outgoing wavefronts can look nearly semicircular.
- A large obstacle can cast a wave shadow. For a small obstacle, waves spread around its edges and fill much of that region.
- To compare ripple-tank results, change either the gap width or the wavelength while holding the other fixed.
### Check yourself
**Q13.** Straight waves with λ = 3 cm approach a barrier. Draw the fronts beyond a 3 cm gap and then beyond a 15 cm gap. Which case spreads through the larger angle?

**Q14.** Why can you hear a person in an adjoining room through an open doorway even if the person is out of sight? Compare the typical wavelengths of sound and visible light with the opening.

**Q15.** Two sounds have frequencies 250 Hz and 1000 Hz. Both travel at 340 m/s. Find their wavelengths and predict which one spreads more noticeably through a 1.0 m gap.

## 6 Superposition and double-slit patterns
### Key ideas
- Add signed displacements where waves overlap. Opposite displacements cancel completely only if their magnitudes are equal.
- Two coherent, in-phase sources give maxima at integer path differences and minima at half-integer path differences. For equal amplitudes the minima can form nodal lines.
- For two narrow slits and a distant screen, d is slit separation, L is the screen distance, and y is the fringe offset from the central maximum.
### Equations and conditions
```
y(resultant) = y₁ + y₂
Maxima: path difference = mλ         Minima: (m + ½)λ
Bright fringes: d sin θ ≈ mλ          For small θ: y ≈ mλL/d
```
### Check yourself
**Q16.** A +4.0 cm displacement overlaps a −2.5 cm displacement. Find the resultant. Describe the pair of displacements needed for complete cancellation.

**Q17.** For two in-phase sources, λ = 0.20 m. Point P is 1.40 m from one source and 1.70 m from the other. Does it lie on a nodal line or a maximum if the amplitudes match?

**Q18.** A double slit has separation 0.250 mm. The first bright band sits 3.60 mm from the central maximum on a screen 1.50 m away. Find the wavelength using the small-angle equation.

## 7 Doppler effect and shock waves
### Key ideas
- An approaching source emits successive fronts from positions closer to the observer, so the observed frequency rises. Behind a receding source, the fronts are farther apart.
- A moving observer can also encounter fronts at a different rate. The source’s own frequency remains the same.
- The **Mach number** is the speed of the source relative to the air divided by the local speed of sound.
- A supersonic object outruns its own sound waves. The resulting shock cone produces a **sonic boom** when it passes an observer.
### Check yourself
**Q19.** A siren moves through still air toward one stationary listener and away from another. Compare observed pitch, crest spacing, and wave speed at the two locations.

**Q20.** You cycle toward a stationary speaker. Does the spacing between crests in the air change? Does the frequency you hear change? Explain.

**Q21.** An aircraft moves at 425 m/s through still air where sound travels at 340 m/s. Find its Mach number. Explain what produces the boom a ground observer hears.

## 8 Answers to questions 1–12
### Wave types and measurements · Q1–3
**1.** One flick makes a **pulse**. Regular oscillation makes a **periodic wave**. The marked rope segment moves transversely while the pattern travels along the rope.

**2.** λ = 0.85 − 0.20 = **0.65 m**. A = **3.0 cm**. The height from trough to crest is 2A = **6.0 cm**.

**3.** The coils oscillate left and right, parallel to the wave’s travel. It is a **longitudinal wave**. Measure from one compression centre to the next.

### Frequency, period, and speed · Q4–6
**4.** f = 18/12.0 = **1.50 Hz**. T = 12.0/18 = **0.667 s**.

**5.** λ = 343/440 ≈ **0.780 m**. At 880 Hz, λ = 343/880 ≈ **0.390 m**.

**6.** f = 30 000 Hz and λ = 0.0500 m. v = fλ = **1500 m/s**. The 0.800 s covers a round trip. The distance is vΔt/2 = **600 m**.

### Reflection · Q7–9
**7.** The normal is perpendicular to the barrier. Incidence and reflection are both 90° − 25° = **65°** to the normal. Draw the two rays on opposite sides of the normal.

**8.** Draw a **downward pulse** leaving the fixed end. At that end, incoming and reflected displacements add to zero so the endpoint stays fixed.

**9.** The reflected fronts are nearly **straight and parallel**, travelling along the axis. Reversed, the fronts reflect and converge at the focus.

### Refraction · Q10–12
**10.** v(deep) = (6.00)(0.0300) = **0.180 m/s**. v(shallow) = (6.00)(0.0200) = **0.120 m/s**. The ratio is **0.667** and f is still **6.00 Hz**.

**11.** It bends **toward the normal** on entry and away on exit. With parallel boundaries and equal outer depths, the final ray is parallel to the original.

**12.** The ray travels **straight through along the normal**. Wavelength decreases. The time between crests remains T because the frequency stays fixed.

## 9 Answers to questions 13–21
### Diffraction · Q13–15
**13.** Draw nearly semicircular fronts spreading from the **3 cm gap**. Behind the 15 cm gap, draw straighter fronts in the centre with curved ends.

**14.** Audible wavelengths can be comparable to the doorway, so sound spreads into the adjacent room. Visible-light wavelengths are much smaller than the opening and spread very little there.

**15.** λ = 340/250 = **1.36 m** and λ = 340/1000 = **0.340 m**. The **250 Hz** wave spreads more because its wavelength is larger relative to the gap.

### Superposition and double-slit patterns · Q16–18
**16.** y = 4.0 − 2.5 = **+1.5 cm**. Complete cancellation requires displacements of equal magnitude and opposite sign at the same location and time.

**17.** Δr = |1.70 − 1.40| = 0.30 m = **1.5λ**. Crest meets trough, so P is on a **nodal line** for equal amplitudes.

**18.** λ ≈ yd/(mL) = (3.60 × 10⁻³)(2.50 × 10⁻⁴)/(1 × 1.50) = **6.00 × 10⁻⁷ m = 600 nm**.

### Doppler effect and shock waves · Q19–21
**19.** The listener ahead encounters **closer crests and higher pitch**. The listener behind encounters **wider spacing and lower pitch**. The wave speed relative to still air is the same.

**20.** Crest spacing in still air is unchanged. You **hear a higher frequency** because you move toward the incoming fronts and meet more of them each second.

**21.** Mach number = 425/340 = **1.25**. The aircraft outruns its sound and creates a shock cone. A ground observer hears the boom as the shock passes.
