                                       The product of the force and the time interval during which it acts is called the 
                                 impulse. The last equation shows that the impulse is equal to the change in momentum 
                                 it produces. 
                                       This is an important relationship when considering an object that is undergoing a 
                                 change in momentum. An object's momentum changes because an impulse has been 
                                 placed on the object. For example  if you are driving down a road at a constant velocity  
                                 you and your vehicle have a momentum. When you press on the gas pedal  the velocity 
                                 of the vehicle increases. The momentum has also increased. This increased momentum 
                                 was caused by a force being exerted on the vehicle over a period of time. Or put another 
                                 way  the vehicle experienced an impulse. The amount of impulse equals the change in 
                                 momentum in the vehicle.

                                 Units for Momentum and Impulse
                                 Momentum is measured in kgtm/s because these units have the dimensions of mass and 
                                 velocity. Impulse is measured in Nts because these units have the dimensions of force and 
                                 time. Since impulse is equal to change in momentum  these units must be equivalent. It 
                                 can easily be shown that this is true:
                                                                  Nts   =   kgtm/s2  t  s   =   kgtm/s
                                       Momentum and impulse may be expressed in either unit.
