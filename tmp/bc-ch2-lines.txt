 48.0 313.0 Free Fall
168.0 300.3 One of the most common situations involving uniform acceleration is the phenomenon 
168.0 313.8 known as free fall. For example  if a coin drops out of your pocket  it accelerates toward 
156.0 319.3  
168.0 327.3 the ground. If the effects of air resistance are ignored  the acceleration of the coin toward 
168.0 340.8 the ground is uniform. The coin starts its downward fall with zero speed  but gains 
168.0 354.3 speed as it falls toward Earth. Since gravity is the cause of the acceleration  we call the 
168.0 367.8 acceleration during free fall the acceleration of gravity. The acceleration of gravity is 
168.0 381.3 given a special symbol  g.
306.2 394.8 The magnitude of g depends on your location. At Earth's 
282.2 407.6 surface  g is approximately 9.81 m/s 2 . At higher altitudes  
282.2 421.8 g decreases. For our present purposes  g is assumed to be 
282.2 434.6 constant at Earth's surface and to be 9.81 m/s 2 . On the Moon  
282.2 448.8 the magnitude of g is approximately 1/6 of what it is here on 
282.2 462.3 Earth's surface. A body in free fall near the Moon's surface has 
282.2 475.1 an acceleration of gravity of only 1.60 m/s 2 .
306.2 489.3 Of course  the four equations for uniform acceleration 
282.2 502.8 apply to free fall as well as other uniform acceleration 
282.2 516.3 situations. The symbol g may be substituted for a in those 
282.2 529.8 equations.
